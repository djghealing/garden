Mission

To offer spiritual healing and temple services that draw on many traditions with care and respect, so that everyone who comes to the garden can meet the sacred in a form that speaks to them.

My lineages

Both the ancient Egyptian and ancient Greek lineages are sustained by my ancestral connections to beings in heavenly realms. My ancient Egyptian teacher is called Nakht-mut, meaning "strength of the water mother," who dwells in the House of Life. My Greek guide is named Tefthras Linios. In their devotion, both developed sufficient spiritual power to become semi-immortal in their lifetimes.

Both may also be invoked with an intention to follow your highest good in Nature, if you feel called to do so.

Tefthras of Linos

My Greek spirit guide, who ensures that my connection to the Greek pantheon holds a direct and correct lineage, is Tefthras of Linos. He asked me to place a song in his name on this page that may be spoken for his heroic blessings, and to cultivate a stronger connection to the sprawling pantheon of the Greeks more widely.

He himself was a priest of Apollo in his lifetime, but, situated in time, was much closer to the Greek religious world in Indra's net of interdependent causes and conditions. Here is this practice:

Candle lit, outside at night, whisper to the stars, with lightness and passion:

Tefthras above
mighty spirit prince
take this frustration
this weight of guilt
this gift for one
who watches clear
and guides us to
gods before

Ma'at, the Dao, and the dharmakaya

Three traditions, three names for the same deep order that all of my work returns to.

Ma'at

In ancient Egypt, Ma'at was truth, balance, and right order, and the goddess who embodies them. The world was held in being by Ma'at, and at death the heart was weighed against her feather. To live in Ma'at is to live in truth with yourself, others, and the gods.

Daoist harmony

The Dao is the way things naturally move. Harmony with it comes not through force but through wuwei, effortless action, letting yin and yang find their own balance. This is why I heal from wuwei rather than from will.

The dharmakaya

In Buddhism, the dharmakaya is the truth body: the formless, unconditioned nature of awakening, open and luminous, and present in every being. In Vajrayana it is the true nature of mind itself, which is never harmed, only obscured.

Whether we call it Ma'at, the Dao, or the dharmakaya, healing is a return to it: clearing what obscures your nature, so that balance, flow, and clarity can arise by themselves.

A syncretic master

With a background in Western analytic philosophy of science and epistemology, as well as finance and law, I have developed a novel syncretic religious worldview embodying both fierce analytical rigour and balance with intuitive insight.

My greatest teachers are William James, Socrates, and Lao Tzu, and Buddhist masters of the Nyingma and Kagyu schools of Vajrayana.

My orientation is firmly on the highest good for the beings of all realms and times.
